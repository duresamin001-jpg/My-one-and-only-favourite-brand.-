Our Heritage · At Home in the UAE

The House of Dure Khan

Born in the embroiderers’ lanes of Lahore’s old walled city and refined between the drape rooms of Dubai Design District and the private salons of Paris, Dure Khan carries heirloom South Asian couture into a contemporary global silhouette.

Chapter One

From the Walled City to the World Stage

Dure Khan began with a single karchob frame and a promise: that the embroiderers of Lahore’s historic lanes would never be anonymous again. Every commission is signed by the master karigar who worked it — the same hands that once dressed Mughal courts now finish garments worn from Dubai to Paris.

The house pairs this heritage with the discipline of Parisian tailoring. Hems are calibrated to the millimeter, panels are cut on the true bias, and every silhouette is balanced against the architectural footwear that anchors it.

Chapter Two

Zardozi, Dabka, and the Patience of Gold

Zardozi is the art of drawing with gold wire. In our ateliers, yards of silk and organza are stretched over timber frames while Ustaads lay dabka coils, French knots, and beaten bullion thread — motifs that can take four hundred hours to complete for a single bridal ensemble.

Each palette is dyed the slow way: turmeric, pomegranate rind, and madder root, steeped by hand for colors that deepen rather than fade with the decades.
